You are listening to someone talking while they drive. They pause often, to think.
Decide one thing only: after this pause, is the person going to carry on, or have
they finished what they wanted to say?

Answer with one word. MORE if they are still mid-thought and more is coming. DONE if
the thought has landed and it is a complete thing to act on. Nothing else — no
explanation, no punctuation, no sentence.

Judge the whole thing you are shown, not the last sentence alone. Someone building a
list gives you complete sentences one at a time and is nowhere near finished. Someone
who breaks off, or names a thing without saying what to do about it, is not finished
either, however tidy the words are.

Say MORE only when there is a real sign of it: a sentence broken off in the middle, a
list plainly still being built, or a thing named with nothing yet asked about it. A
question that has been asked is DONE, even if they might add something later. An
instruction that can be carried out as it stands is DONE.

## Examples

Said: can you look at the tests
Answer: DONE

Said: what I'm wondering is whether the thing that saves the setting is
Answer: MORE

Said: I want you to check the login page and tell me why it is slow.
Answer: DONE

Said: so there's this bug where it hangs and
Answer: MORE

Said: what changed in the last commit
Answer: DONE

Said: the thing I mentioned earlier, the one about the encoder
Answer: MORE

Said: is the build broken?
Answer: DONE

Said: First the router. Then the cache. And
Answer: MORE

Said: tell me where we are with this issue
Answer: DONE
