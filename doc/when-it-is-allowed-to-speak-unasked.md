# When it is allowed to speak unasked

Nearly everything this app says was asked for: an answer, a confirmation, a question
of its own. Two things are not. It reminds you that something is still running, and it
tells you when something has finished. Nobody asked for either, and nobody is waiting
on either.

They were spoken the moment they arrived. That was wrong in a way that took a while to
see, because it looks like ordinary rudeness and it is not.

## Interrupting a sentence here deletes it

Words only reach this app at the pauses. While a sentence is still being spoken it
exists nowhere but in the ear that is hearing it — nothing has been written down, and
there is nothing on the screen to go back to.

Separately, everything arriving while this app is talking is thrown away. It has to be:
almost all of it is the app's own voice coming back off the windscreen, and the two
attempts at telling the difference by comparing words both leaked, in both directions.

Put those together and an announcement landing mid-sentence does not interrupt a
sentence. It deletes one — all of it, back to the last pause. The longer the sentence,
the more goes. From the driver's seat it looks like the app cutting in and then losing
everything you had just said, which is exactly what it is.

## The rule

Nothing said on this app's own account goes out when it arrives.

1. **It is written to the screen immediately.** Only the speaking waits. Nothing is
   ever lost by holding it back.
2. **It waits for a real break** — nothing being said, no words arriving, no half of a
   phrase still hanging. The break has to be longer when there is an unsent question on
   the screen: that is somebody mid-thought with the rest of the sentence still in their
   head, and a pause in the middle of dictating is not an invitation.
3. **At least a minute between any two of them**, whatever produced them. Several
   separate things can each have something to announce and without a floor they queue up
   behind one break and all arrive together.
4. **Only the newest is kept while waiting.** Two of these are usually the same sentence
   with a bigger number in it. Saying an old one late is worse than not saying it: it
   describes a state of the world nobody is in any more.
5. **Something nobody could be told for a long time is dropped**, not saved up.
6. **A question going off drops whatever was waiting.** Asking something new says as
   plainly as anything can that the moment the announcement was about has passed.
7. **If one lands while somebody is talking anyway, the person wins.** The app stops
   talking and keeps the words, instead of discarding them as its own echo. A break can
   end the instant an announcement starts, so this can still happen; it just must not
   cost anything when it does.

## Why it is a rule about a kind of thing, not about the reminder

The reminder about work still running is what exposed this, but it has nothing
special about it. The notice that something has finished has identical manners and
would do identical damage, and so will the next one. So the holding is a single thing
that every unasked-for sentence goes through, in both front ends — the free one and the
paid one, which cuts a turn off just as thoroughly when it is handed something to say.

## Where it lives

The holding itself is one small piece in the page with no opinions about voices or
ears: it is given a way to ask whether now is a good moment and a way to say something,
and it decides when. Each front end supplies its own notion of "a good moment", because
what counts as somebody being mid-sentence is genuinely different between the two.

The three numbers — the two breaks and the floor — are settings on the Mac, so they can
be changed without touching the page. The judgement itself cannot move to the Mac: the
phone is the only thing that knows whether somebody is in the middle of a sentence.

Exercised against a clock we control rather than only in a car, including the case that
first hid itself: a clock starting near zero used to read "nothing said yet" as "said a
moment ago" and hold the very first announcement back for a full minute.
